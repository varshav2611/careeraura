10 skills worth building either way

From the World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025 — based on over 1,000 employers across 55 economies, on what will matter most through 2030.

  1. Analytical thinking — breaking a problem into pieces and reasoning through it. The single most-valued skill for the third report running.
  2. Resilience, flexibility & agility — staying steady and adapting when plans change.
  3. Leadership & social influence — bringing people together and guiding a group, even informally.
  4. Creative thinking — generating genuinely new ideas and approaches, not just following a template.
  5. Motivation & self-awareness — knowing what drives you and staying self-directed without needing to be pushed.
  6. Technological literacy — comfort learning and using new tools quickly, especially AI.
  7. Empathy & active listening — genuinely understanding someone else's perspective before responding.
  8. Curiosity & lifelong learning — staying a learner by habit, not just when required to.
  9. Talent management — helping others grow — relevant even early on, through mentoring or leading peers.
  10. Service orientation — genuinely caring about the person you're serving, whatever the role.

The report also flags AI & big data, networks & cybersecurity, and technological literacy as the fastest-growing technical skills specifically — worth layering on top of whichever path you choose above.

Source: World Economic Forum, Future of Jobs Report 2025

Specifically, in India right now

What Indian employers are actually hiring for

From the India Skills Report 2026 (ETS, CII, AICTE, AIU & Taggd) and NASSCOM.

  1. AI & Machine Learning — the single largest gap in Indian tech hiring right now; NASSCOM estimates roughly a 50%+ demand-supply gap.
  2. Data Analytics & Data Science — growing alongside AI as one of the fastest-hiring categories nationally.
  3. Cloud Computing — the infrastructure behind India's fastest-growing sectors, from telecom to biotech.
  4. Cybersecurity — rising fast as India's digital infrastructure scales.
  5. Fluency with GenAI tools — over 90% of Indian employees already use generative AI at work; this is now a baseline expectation, not a bonus.
  6. Remote & hybrid work readiness — Indian workplaces are shifting fast toward flexible, digital-first setups.
  7. Gig & freelance-readiness — India's gig workforce is projected to hit 23.5 million by 2030; managing your own projects and clients is an increasingly normal career skill, not a fallback.
  8. Renewable energy & sustainability skills — a named growth sector alongside tech, BFSI, manufacturing, and healthcare.
  9. Entrepreneurial thinking — explicitly named a 2026 workforce theme, valuable even inside a traditional job.
  10. Skills-first adaptability — Indian hiring is shifting from degree-first to skills-first; being able to show what you can do matters as much as what you studied.

Sectors leading hiring in 2026: Technology, BFSI, Manufacturing, Renewable Energy, and Healthcare. Highest-employability states: Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, and Karnataka.

Source: India Skills Report 2026 (ETS, CII, AICTE, AIU & Taggd) & NASSCOM

Where India actually discusses this

The most authoritative forum on future skills in India is nasscom FutureSkills and its learner-facing platform FutureSkills Prime — a MeitY-backed national initiative, free to join, with government incentives (up to ₹14,500) for completing certain courses.

  1. FutureSkills Prime — India's formal technology skilling hub: certifications, bootcamps, and direct sessions with industry experts, aligned to national skill standards (NSQF).
  2. nasscom FutureSkills Community — the professional network for tech trends and workforce strategy discussions.
  3. Bharat Skills Forum — a government (DGT) platform for vocational training, ITIs, and industrial skills — useful beyond just tech-degree paths.

For the policy/big-picture side: the FICCI Global Skills Summit and WEF's India Skills Accelerator are where industry leaders and policymakers shape national skilling strategy — worth knowing about even if not something a student attends directly.

Source: nasscom FutureSkills, FutureSkills Prime (MeitY-backed), Bharat Skills Forum (DGT)
